Performance Portability of the Chapel Language on Heterogeneous Architectures [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Josh Milthorpe, Xianghao Wang, Ahmad Azizi, Heterogeneity in Computing Workshop (HCW 2024) at IPDPS 2024, May 27, 2024

This paper uses mini-applications to compare the performance portability of Chapel with OpenMP, Kokkos, CUDA, and HIP across a wide range of CPU and GPU platforms.

A performance-oriented comparative study of the Chapel high-productivity language to conventional programming environments [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Guillaume Helbecque, Jan Gmys, Tiago Carneiro, Nouredine Melab, Pascal Bouvry, 13th International Workshop on Programming Models and Applications for Multicores and Manycores (PMAM 2022), Seoul, South Korea, April 2, 2022

This work compares the performance of a Chapel-based fractal generation on shared- and distributed-memory platforms with corresponding OpenMP and MPI+X implementations.

A Comparative Study of High-Productivity High-Performance Programming Languages for Parallel Metaheuristics [ Paper | PDF ] 🔗
Jan Gmys, Tiago Carneiro, Nouredine Melab, El-Ghazali Talbi, Daniel Tuyttens, Swarm and Evolutionary Computation, volume 57, September 2020

This paper compares Chapel with Julia, Python/Numba, and C+OpenMP in terms of performance, scalability and productivity. Two parallel metaheuristics are implemented for solving the 3D Quadratic Assignment Problem (Q3AP), using thread-based parallelism on a multi-core shared-memory computer. The paper also evaluates and compares the performance of the languages for a parallel fitness evaluation loop, using four different test functions with different computational characteristics. The authors provide feedback on the implementation and parallelization process in each language.

Development of a Knowledge-Sharing Parallel Computing Approach for Calibrating Distributed Watershed Hydrologic Models [ Paper ] 🔗
Marjan Asgari, Wanhong Yang, John Lindsay, Hui Shao, Yongbo Liu, Rodrigo De Queiroga Miranda, and Maryam Mehri Dehnavi, Environmental Modelling & Software, volume 164, June 2023

This paper uses Chapel in a novel knowledge-sharing setting to support a general parallel framework for calibrating distributed hydrologic models. The approach is unique due to the use of a novel search algorithm as well as its interoperability with C#, fault tolerance, parallelism, and reliability.

Computational Fluid Dynamics

Development of Parallel CFD Applications with the Chapel Programming Language [ Paper | Slides | Video ] 🔗
Matthieu Parenteau, Simon Bourgault-Côté, Frédéric Plante, Engin Kayraklioglu, Éric Laurendeau, AIAA Scitech 2021 Forum, January 13, 2021

This paper describes a Computational Fluid Dynamics framework being developed using Chapel by a team at Polytechnique Montreal. The use of Chapel is described, and scaling results are given on up to 9k cores of a Cray XC. Comparisons are made against well-established CFD software packages.

Data and Graph Analysis

VF2-PS: Parallel and Scalable Subgraph Monomorphism in Arachne [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Mohammad Dindoost, Oliver Alvarado Rodriguez, Sounak Bagchi, Palina Pauliuchenka, Zhihui Du, David Bader, IEEE HPEC 2024, September 24, 2024

This paper introduces VF2-PS, a parallel and scalable implementation of the VF2 algorithm for subgraph monomorphism, developed in Chapel and integrated into the Arachne framework, demonstrating speedups of up to 97.0× on 128 cores compared to existing sequential Python-based tools.

A Deployment Tool for Large Scale Graph Analytics Framework Arachne [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Garrett Gonzalez-Rivas, Zhihui Du, David Bader, IEEE HPEC 2024, September 24, 2024

The paper presents a deployment tool designed to simplify the installation process for Arachne, a large-scale, Arkouda-based graph analytics framework, reducing setup time from over a week to a significantly shorter period while improving efficiency for both users and developers.

Search and Branch-and-Bound

Efficient and scalable branch-and-bound algorithm for exact qubit allocation [ Paper ] 🔗
Jean-Philippe Valois, Guillaume Helbecque, Nouredine Melab, Future Generation Computer Systems, volume 179, June 2026

This paper uses a Chapel-based parallel branch-and-bound framework for exactly solving challenging qubit allocation instances. Experimental results show near-linear intra-node scaling and strong multi-node performance, and deliver new reference optimal solutions for benchmark circuits of up to 26 qubits, exceeding previously reported instances.

Investigating Portability in Chapel for Tree-based Optimization on GPU-powered Clusters [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Tiago Carneiro, Engin Kayraklioglu, Guillaume Helbecque, Nouredine Melab, Euro-PAR 2024, August 28, 2024

This paper investigates the viability of using Chapel as a tool to achieve both code and performance portability in large-scale tree-based search for solving permutation combinatorial problems. Extensive experiments using up to 512 NVIDIA GPUs and 1024 AMD GPUs show that it is possible to scale on the two different systems using the same tree-based search written in Chapel.

PGAS Data Structure for Unbalanced Tree-Based Algorithms at Scale [ Paper | Slides | Preprint ] 🔗
Guillaume Helbecque, Tiago Carneiro, Nouredine Melab, Jan Gmys, and Pascal Bouvry, International Conference on Computational Science (ICCS 2024), July 2–4, 2024

This paper introduces a PGAS-based data structure and work-stealing mechanism for parallel depth-first traversal of unbalanced trees, implemented as the Chapel DistributedBag module.

GPU-Accelerated Tree-Search in Chapel versus CUDA and HIP [ Paper | Slides | Preprint ] 🔗
Guillaume Helbecque, Ezhilmathi Krishnasamy, Nouredine Melab, Pascal Bouvry, PDCO 2024 at IPDPS 2024, May 31, 2024

This paper presents a proof-of-concept GPU-accelerated tree search algorithm in Chapel, showcasing a portable PGAS-based approach to parallel backtracking on both Nvidia and AMD GPU architectures.

Parallel distributed productivity-aware tree-search using Chapel [ Paper | Preprint ] 🔗
Guillaume Helbecque, Jan Gmys, Nouredine Melab, Tiago Carneiro, and Pascal Bouvry, Concurrency and Computation: Practice and Experience, July 19, 2023

This paper presents P3D-DFS, a framework for parallel distributed tree search algorithms implemented in Chapel using the distBag data structure, demonstrating Chapel's viability as a productive and performant alternative to OpenMP and MPI+X in exascale computing.

A Local Search for Automatic Parameterization of Distributed Tree Search Algorithms [ Paper ] 🔗
Tiago Carneiro, Loizos Koutsantonis, Nouredine Melab, Emmanuel Kieffer, Pascal Bouvry, 12th IEEE Workshop Parallel / Distributed Combinatorics and Optimization (PDCO 2022), June 3, 2022

This work presents a local search for automatic parameterization of ChapelBB, a distributed tree search application for solving combinatorial optimization problems written in Chapel. The main objective of the proposed heuristic is to overcome the limitation of manual parameterization, which covers a limited feasible space.

Towards ultra-scale Branch-and-Bound using a high-productivity language [ Paper ] 🔗
Tiago Carneiro, Jan Gmys, Nouredine Melab, and Daniel Tuyttens, Future Generation Computer Systems, volume 105, pages 196-209, April 2020

This paper uses Chapel to study the design and implementation of distributed Branch-and-Bound algorithms for solving large combinatorial optimization problems. Experiments on the proposed algorithms are performed using the Flow-shop scheduling problem as a test-case. The Chapel-based application is compared to a state-of-the-art MPI+Pthreads-based counterpart in terms of performance, scalability, and productivity.

Towards Chapel-based Exascale Tree Search Algorithms: dealing with multiple GPU accelerators [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Tiago Carneiro, Nouredine Melab, Akihiro Hayashi, Vivek Sarkar, HPCS 2020, Outstanding Paper Award winner, March 22–27, 2021

This paper revisits the design and implementation of tree search algorithms dealing with multiple GPUs, in addition to scalability and productivity-awareness using Chapel. The proposed algorithm exploits Chapel's distributed iterators by combining a partial search strategy with pre-compiled CUDA kernels for more efficient exploitation of the intra-node parallelism.

Graph Algorithms in PGAS: Chapel and UPC++ [ Paper ] 🔗
Louis Jenkins, Jesun Sahariar Firoz, Marcin Zalewski, Cliff Joslyn, Mark Raugas, 2019 IEEE High Performance Extreme Computing Conference (HPEC ‘19), September 24–26, 2019

This paper compares implementations of Breadth-First Search and Triangle Counting in Chapel and UPC++

Chapel Overviews and History

Chapel chapter [ PDF ] 🔗
Bradford L. Chamberlain, Programming Models for Parallel Computing, edited by Pavan Balaji, published by MIT Press, November 2015

This is currently the best introduction to Chapel's history, motivating themes, and features. It also provides a brief summary of current and future activities at the time of writing. An early pre-print of this chapter was made available under the name A Brief Overview of Chapel.

Chapel Comes of Age: Productive Parallelism at Scale [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Brad Chamberlain, Elliot Ronaghan, Ben Albrecht, Lydia Duncan, Michael Ferguson, Ben Harshbarger, David Iten, David Keaton, Vassily Litvinov, Preston Sahabu, and Greg Titus, CUG 2018, Stockholm Sweden, May 22, 2018

This paper describes the progress that has been made with Chapel since the HPCS program wrapped up.

The State of the Chapel Union [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Bradford L. Chamberlain, Sung-Eun Choi, Martha Dumler, Thomas Hildebrandt, David Iten, Vassily Litvinov, Greg Titus. , CUG 2013, Napa California, May 2013

This paper provides a snapshot of the Chapel project at the juncture between the end of the HPCS project and the start of the next phase in Chapel's development. It covers past successes, current status, and future directions.

Parallel Programmability and the Chapel Language [ Paper ] 🔗
Bradford L. Chamberlain, David Callahan, Hans P. Zima, International Journal of High Performance Computing Applications, August 2007, 21(3): 291-312, August 2007

This is an early overview of Chapel's themes and main language concepts.

The Cascade High Productivity Language [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
David Callahan, Bradford L. Chamberlain, Hans P. Zima, In 9th International Workshop on High-Level Parallel Programming Models and Supportive Environments (HIPS 2004), pages 52-60. IEEE Computer Society, April 26, 2004

This is the original Chapel paper which lays out some of our motivation and foundations for exploring the language. Note that the language has evolved significantly since this paper was published, but it remains an interesting historical artifact.

Chapel Implementation and Optimizations

Productive, Vendor-Neutral GPU Programming Using Chapel [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Engin Kayraklioglu, Andy Stone, WACCPD at SC24, November 18, 2024

This paper introduces Chapel's GPU support. It provides a brief background on Chapel's parallelism and locality concepts, as well as how they relate to GPU programming. A real-world application with results from Frontier is also covered. However, the main focus of the paper is how the GPU support is implemented in the compiler, runtime and internal modules.

Adaptive Prefetching for Fine-grain Communication in PGAS Programs [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Thomas B. Rolinger, Alan Sussman, IPDPS 2024, May 30, 2024

This paper presents a compiler optimization that targets irregular memory access patterns in Chapel programs. The optimization employs prefetching into Chapel's remote data cache for irregular memory access patterns and will dynamically adjust the prefetching behavior as the program executes.

Compiler Optimization for Irregular Memory Access Patterns in PGAS Programs [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Thomas B. Rolinger, Christopher D. Krieger, Alan Sussman, LCPC 2022, October 13, 2022

This paper presents a compiler optimization that targets irregular memory accesses patterns in Chapel programs. Specifically, it uses static analysis to identify irregular memory accesses to distributed arrays in parallel loops and employs code transformations to generate an inspector and executor to perform selective data replication at runtime.

Locality-Based Optimizations in the Chapel Compiler [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Engin Kayraklioglu, Elliot Ronaghan, Michael P. Ferguson, and Bradford L. Chamberlain, LCPC 2021, October 13, 2021

This paper describes a pair of recent compiler optimizations focused on reducing communication overheads in Chapel, leveraging Chapel's high-level abstractions—one that strength reduces local array accesses, and a second which aggregates communications to amortize overheads.

A Machine-Learning-Based Framework for Productive Locality Exploitation [ Paper ] 🔗
Engin Kayraklioglu, Erwan Fawry, Tarek El-Ghazawi, IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems (IEEE TPDS). Volume 32, Issue 6, June 2021

This paper describes an approach that can efficiently train machine learning models that can be used to improve application execution times and scalability on distributed memory systems. This is achieved by analyzing the fine-grained communication profile of the application with small input data, and then predicting the communication patterns for more realistic inputs and coarsening the communication.

LLVM-based Communication Optimizations for PGAS Programs [ Paper ] 🔗
Akihiro Hayashi, Jisheng Zhao, Michael Ferguson, Vivek Sarkar, 2nd Workshop on the LLVM Compiler Infrastructure in HPC (LLVM-HPC2), November 2015

This paper describes how LLVM passes can optimize communication in PGAS languages like Chapel. In particular, by representing potentially remote addresses using a distinct address space, existing LLVM optimization passes can be used to reduce communication

Caching Puts and Gets in a PGAS Language Runtime [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Michael P. Ferguson, Daniel Buettner, 9th International Conference on Partitioned Global Address Space Programming Models (PGAS 2015), Sept 2015

This paper describes an optimization implemented for Chapel in which the runtime library aggregates puts and gets in accordance with Chapel's memory consistency model in order to reduce the potential overhead of doing fine-grained communications.

Multiresolution Chapel Features

User-Defined Parallel Zippered iterators in Chapel [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Bradford L. Chamberlain, Sung-Eun Choi, Steven J. Deitz, Angeles Navarro, PGAS 2011: Fifth Conference on Partitioned Global Address Space Programming Models, October 2011

This paper describes how users can create parallel iterators that support zippered iteration in Chapel, demonstrating them via several examples that partition iteration spaces statically and dynamically.

Authoring User-Defined Domain Maps in Chapel [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Bradford L. Chamberlain, Sung-Eun Choi, Steven J. Deitz, David Iten, Vassily Litvinov, CUG 2011, May 2011

This paper builds on our HotPAR 2010 paper by describing the programmer's role in implementing user-defined distributions and layouts in Chapel.

User-Defined Distributions and Layouts in Chapel: Philosophy and Framework [ Paper | Slides ] 🔗
Bradford L. Chamberlain, Steven J. Deitz, David Iten, Sung-Eun Choi, 2nd USENIX Workshop on Hot Topics in Parallelism (HotPar'10), June 2010

This paper describes our approach and software framework for implementing user-defined distributions and memory layouts using Chapel's domain map concept.
